## Notes and limits

- CHIRPS is not a ground measurement: it blends satellite and stations. According to its README, CHIRPS v3 already blends INSIVUMEH stations (https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/products/CHIRPS/v3.0/README-CHIRPSv3.0.txt), so an independent comparison against stations is still missing.
- POWER: 0.5° × 0.625° cell (~50 km). CHIRPS: 0.05° (~5 km).
- The engine's rain is the CHIRPS monthly total split across days with POWER.
- High risk = the year's deficit is above the 75th percentile of the previous years (from 2001).
- NDVI signal = July-September anomaly of -1 standard deviation or less.
- SMAP 2018 anomaly = July-August 2018 against the other SMAP years (since 2015, including years after 2018). SMAP is not part of the “what was known up to 2017” run: that run uses only the water deficit with CHIRPS rain.
- SMAP: SPL3SMP_E.006 through AppEEARS, recommended-quality data only. Camotán has none; Jocotán and San Juan Ermita share the 9 km cell. SMAP measures the top 0-5 cm of soil and describes about 9 km of landscape, not the plot.
- The 5 plots are example points chosen with NDVI and Sentinel-2 imagery, not verified in the field; see [es/estado.md](es/estado.md).
- The canícula fact depends on how CHIRPS splits rain by day: the comparison of “sat” (IMERG) with “rnl” (ERA5) is in [canicula-sensitivity.md](canicula-sensitivity.md).
