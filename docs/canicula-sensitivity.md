## What was tested

The traffic light's «canícula» (midsummer drought) fact counts the years with a run of more than 15 days under 2 mm between 15 June and 31 August, using CHIRPS v3 daily “sat” (each CHIRPS pentad split across days with NASA IMERG). The Climate Hazards Center warns that dry spells can differ substantially with “rnl” (the same pentad split with ERA5) and asks users to test both (https://data.chc.ucsb.edu/products/CHIRPS/v3.0/daily/readme.txt). Here the engine's series is built twice per plot, changing only the daily cache, with the same functions and the same cut-offs.

## Checks

- With “sat” the script reproduces the counts the site publishes (Jocotán 2, Camotán 2, Olopa 0, San Juan Ermita 1, Zacapa 3, of 25 years) and the same spell year by year as `web/public/data/`; otherwise it writes nothing.
- Both versions split the same pentad: each June-August month's rainfall differs by at most 0.05 mm between “sat” and “rnl” (rounding of the daily values). This confirms the same pixel and period were read.
- Years with a spell of 14 to 17 days in either version (one or two days from the cut-off): Jocotán 2018, 2021; Camotán 2018, 2025; Olopa 2018, 2021; San Juan Ermita 2001, 2015, 2021; Zacapa 2001, 2003, 2004, 2013.

## Limits

- “sat” and “rnl” are not independent: both split the same CHIRPS pentad total and differ only in how they spread it across days (CHC readme). Agreement does not validate the dry spell; it only says the result does not depend on the split chosen.
- Neither is a daily ground measurement. ERA5 (~0.25°) and IMERG (0.1°) are interpolated to the 0.05° grid: the daily split has no 5 km detail.
- When ERA5 or IMERG is zero for the whole pentad and CHIRPS is not, the CHC spreads the total evenly across the days, which can create artificial wet days that break a dry spell.
- Days are UTC calendar days, not Guatemala local time. The cut-off (> 15 days under 2 mm, 15 Jun-31 Aug) is the engine's and was not changed for this test.
- Only June-August rainfall changes; the rest of the year and temperature are the same in both series. The result does not enter any score, the confidence or the audio: the published traffic light still uses “sat”.
- The plots are example points chosen with satellite data, not verified in the field.

```

python -m pipeline.validacion.sensibilidad_canicula
```
